# Worked exercise | The $2,400 transfer

**All facts and parties are invented.** This is an ordinary Québec civil study problem. No tax characterization, session evidence, or advice about any real proceeding is implied. See the [Evidence module](evidence-module.md) and the [official C.C.Q. Book Seven text](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/CCQ-1991?langCont=en).

## Record

Camille claims repayment of $2,400 from Morgan, saying they made an oral personal loan repayable July 31. Morgan admits receiving the $2,400 but says it was a contribution to shared moving expenses with no repayment promise. Neither claims to have been acting in the ordinary course of an enterprise.

- **A. Bank records:** Camille’s bank-origin PDF shows a May 3 completed debit of $2,400 labelled “MOVE,” with reference T42. Morgan’s bank-origin PDF records a $2,400 credit the same day with reference T42. Both parties accept the two statements’ origin and contents. The bank records contain no repayment terms.
- **B. Message:** Camille has a cropped image showing “I’ll send it back by July 31.” The image does not display the sender or surrounding thread, and Morgan contests whether it relates to this transfer. Camille says it came from Morgan. The native conversation has not yet been provided.
- **C. Testimony:** Camille offers in-court personal testimony about the alleged oral promise. Morgan offers in-court testimony that no promise was made.
- **D. Expense record:** Morgan has an invoice from a moving company for $4,800 around the same time. The invoice names Morgan but does not name Camille or identify reference T42. Assume for the exercise that its origin is established, not that every inference from the invoice is correct.

### Try it first

1. What fact is agreed? What juridical act and terms remain disputed? Who must prove the asserted right, and to what standard?
2. Do the bank records establish a loan, a contribution or neither? What does the invoice add?
3. Can Camille automatically prove the oral loan by testimony? What further records could affect art. 2862?
4. Suppose a complete, reliably attributed conversation shows Morgan replying to Camille’s message “I just sent the $2,400 as a loan” with “Received. I’ll pay back the $2,400 by July 31.” Reassess without assuming the court must accept the claim.
5. If Morgan instead admits the loan but contends it was already repaid, which fact must Morgan prove?

## Answer key: one supportable analysis

| Item | Purpose | What the item supports | What it does not show / next check |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Trace the movement | Matching date, amount and reference, plus Morgan’s admission, establish transfer and receipt for the exercise. | The “MOVE” label does not establish the parties’ agreement or the nature of the funds. Identify who entered the label if its significance is disputed. |
| B | Connect Morgan’s words to the transfer and repayment term | A statement about returning something, if attributable and contextualized; the cropped image alone is weak and its use is contestable. | Native thread or authenticated export may identify sender, date, reference and subject. Assess origin, integrity and the applicable original/copy rule separately (arts. 2835, 2837–2842, 2860, as applicable). |
| C | Establish whether an agreement was made and its terms | First-hand observations are potentially relevant, but admissibility depends on what each witness is offered to prove. | With $2,400 in dispute, neither side can assume testimony alone is admissible to prove the alleged juridical act between the parties. Start at art. 2862, examine arts. 2861 and 2865 where raised, and distinguish direct testimony about surrounding facts from proof of the agreement itself. |
| D | Test alternate explanation | The invoice makes a contribution plausible, especially with the “MOVE” label and the 50% match. | It does not allocate the bill or rule out a loan connected to moving. Trace any payment of the invoice and look for contemporaneous terms about sharing costs. Arts. 2846 and 2849 discipline the inference. |

**First pass:** The bank movement is established on the stipulated facts. Camille bears the art. 2803 burden to prove the supporting facts of the loan claim, including the repayment obligation and relevant terms, on the art. 2804 standard. Morgan’s denial accompanied by an alternative account does not itself transfer that burden. The cropped image neither securely attributes the statement nor ties it to T42; the invoice and reference do not themselves establish a contribution. On this record, mark the asserted loan **not established on the materials supplied**, not “proved to be a contribution.” The exact admissibility of the image and the testimony cannot be decided from the hypothetical alone.

**Variation with the complete thread:** If reliably attributed to Morgan and produced in an appropriate form, the reply materially strengthens the loan and due-date inference in context. Depending on its form and use, examine direct written proof and any extrajudicial admission (arts. 2850, 2852 and 2867), or alternatively whether the adverse party’s writing is commencement of proof that makes the loan plausible and opens the testimony route (arts. 2862, 2865). Do not reason in a circle by assuming an alleged admission proves itself. Read the whole exchange rather than extracting one favourable phrase. The starting record had an unexplained label and an invoice; the added message now directly links receipt, purpose and repayment. The court still determines admissibility and weight on the complete record.

**Repayment variation:** If the loan is admitted and Morgan asserts repayment, Morgan bears the burden to prove the facts supporting its extinction (art. 2803, para. 2). A later debit with no matching credit or acknowledgment would require a fresh evidentiary bridge to this debt.

**Smoke-test pass condition:** An answer separates movement from legal characterization, identifies both burden allocations, applies the $1,500 rule to proof of the juridical act rather than to all witnesses, handles the cropped image as a limited source, and names one record that could move the result. No real bank data or actual legal determination is needed.
