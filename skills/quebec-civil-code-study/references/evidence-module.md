# Evidence | Book Seven | Study module v0.1

**Scope:** Québec civil evidence, not a recommendation about an active dispute. This module teaches a repeatable method for moving from an allegation to an evidentiary conclusion. Start with the governing rights and obligations; do not infer them simply from the existence of a document.

**Primary text:** [Civil Code of Québec, Book Seven, arts. 2803–2874.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/CCQ-1991?langCont=en), official English consolidation updated October 15, 2026 when checked. The [French text](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/CCQ-1991) is also available. Check the version applicable to the facts and proceedings, and whether another statute, the procedural track or a court order changes the analysis.

## How to use this module (20–30 minutes)

1. **Frame the issue (3 minutes).** State the right asserted and the disputed material fact in one sentence. Distinguish the transaction from what it legally meant. Mark each fact as agreed, disputed or unknown.
2. **Read the rule (6 minutes).** Read arts. 2803–2805, 2811 and 2857. Then read the article governing the proposed means of proof and any exception it invokes. State who bears the burden and why.
3. **Build a proof table (6 minutes).** For each item complete: *material fact → source → purpose offered → applicable rule → what it supports → what it cannot show → best next check*. Distinguish acceptance into evidence, attribution, integrity, and strength; none guarantees the others.
4. **Try the case (5 minutes).** Answer the prompts in [The $2,400 transfer](fictional-bank-transfer.md) before reading the answer key. Identify the strongest alternate explanation.
5. **State a bounded result (3 minutes).** Say which fact is established or merely supported on the given record; explain why a competing reading survives or fails. Name the next source that would change that conclusion.

## Rules to know

| Question | C.C.Q. pinpoint | Working explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Who must prove the fact? To what standard? | Arts. 2803–2805 | A person asserting a right proves its supporting facts; a person asserting that an existing right is null, modified or extinguished proves the facts supporting that contention. The ordinary civil standard is more probable than not unless law demands more. Good faith is presumed unless the law expressly requires proof. |
| Does it need proof at all? | Arts. 2806–2810 | Judicial notice covers applicable Québec law, with the pleading qualifications in art. 2807, and facts whose notoriety makes their existence reasonably indisputable. Non-Québec law and facts in issue do not automatically qualify; see arts. 2809–2810. |
| Which means of proof and which category of writing? | Arts. 2811–2836 | Book Seven distinguishes writings, testimony, presumptions, admissions and real evidence. For writings, distinguish authentic (2813 ff.), semi-authentic (2822 ff.), signed private (2826–2830), and other writings (2831–2836). Arts. 2828–2829 address proof, acknowledgment and effect of a private writing. An unsigned writing regularly used in ordinary business to evidence a juridical act may qualify under art. 2831; general writings relating facts are treated under art. 2832, subject to Book Seven. Under art. 2835 the proponent of an unsigned writing establishes its origin. Do not classify every bank printout or text message the same way. |
| What changes for digital documents? | Arts. 2837–2842, 2860; [C-1.1](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/C-1.1?langCont=en), ss. 5–7, 12, 16–17 | Medium alone does not disqualify a writing. Integrity, origin, and equivalence to an original are separate. Art. 2840 addresses when proof that a system safeguards integrity is required; it does not prove who authored a message or that its contents are true. Retain native records and full context; whether a reproduction legally stands in for the original depends on the applicable copy/transfer rules. Art. 2839 allows a possible alternative evidentiary role in appropriate circumstances when the medium cannot confirm or deny integrity. |
| What about people, inferences and admissions? | Arts. 2843–2856 | Testimony normally rests on personal knowledge or expert opinion; the court assesses its weight (2843–2845). Distinguish legal presumptions from fact-based inferences; the latter must meet the serious, precise and concordant standard (2846–2849). Admissions in the proceeding and outside it differ in effect (2850–2853; 2867); silence alone generally is not an admission. Real evidence enables direct observation by the judge; check authenticity and the specific digital-document qualification (2854–2856). |
| Is the evidence admissible for this purpose? | Arts. 2857–2859 | Relevance is the starting point, not the whole test. Art. 2858 addresses evidence obtained in violation of fundamental rights and freedoms and the administration-of-justice criterion, with a professional-secrecy qualification. Art. 2858.1 has special rules for matters containing allegations of sexual or spousal violence. Art. 2859 governs when the court cannot raise an objection under its Chapter II on its own initiative. |
| Can testimony prove or change the agreement? | Arts. 2860–2868 | If proving the contents of a writing or the juridical act it sets out, generally produce the original or legal equivalent (2860), subject to the stated good-faith/diligence exception; see also valid inability to procure written proof (2861). Between the parties, testimony generally cannot prove a juridical act where value in dispute exceeds $1,500 (2862); consider the exceptions for commencement of proof where written proof is absent, and for a juridical act proved against the person who executed it in the ordinary course of business of an enterprise. The limit does not exclude all testimony about surrounding facts. Arts. 2863–2864 distinguish contradicting or varying terms from interpreting/completing a clearly incomplete writing or impugning validity. Art. 2865 identifies what may count as commencement of proof and requires that it make the alleged fact plausible; plausibility is not proof of the whole case. |
| What if the speaker is not testifying or the statement was made earlier? | Arts. 2869–2874 | Identify whether a statement is offered for truth, its existence, or another permitted purpose, and whether it is a party admission. Consent or a statutory route can admit qualifying statements as testimony (2869). For an absent witness under art. 2870, check application and notice, court authorization, unavailability or unreasonableness of appearance, and reliability. Ordinary-business and other listed circumstances can supply presumed reliability, not automatic admission. Art. 2871 addresses sufficiently reliable prior statements of a person who testifies; arts. 2872–2874 govern how to prove statements and recordings. |

**Special case outside the worked exercise:** art. 2874.1 addresses proof of fault using a final guilty judgment in an action for damages resulting from an act that constitutes a criminal offence. Do not use it to infer the other elements of civil liability.

### Procedure is a separate check

The [Code of Civil Procedure, C-25.01](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/pdf/cs/C-25.01%20.pdf), especially arts. 247–250 on exhibits and evidence art. 264 on formal notice to admit origin or integrity, and art. 265 on trial, is not Book Seven. Check the applicable case protocol, track, court order and rules before relying on any general timeline. Disclosure to the other side, filing in the record, and admission in evidence are different events. This module does not assess limitation periods, tax treatment, privileged collection, court-specific filing requirements or actual likely outcome.
