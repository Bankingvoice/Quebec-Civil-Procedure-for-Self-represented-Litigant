# Civil Code of Québec: ten-book study map

**One code, ten books.** The *Civil Code of Québec* (*Code civil du Québec*, C.c.Q./C.C.Q.; CQLR, c. CCQ‑1991) supplies general rules of Québec civil law. The *Code of Civil Procedure* (*Code de procédure civile*, C.p.c./C.C.P.; CQLR, c. C‑25.01) is a separate code. Ask what creates or defines the right or obligation, how the disputed facts can be proved, and what procedure governs invoking the right. The books are navigation boundaries, not a claim that every numbered provision in each span is operative.

**Official sources:** [LégisQuébec, English PDF](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/pdf/cs/CCQ-1991.pdf) (554 pages, consolidation marked updated October 15, 2026; checked November 22, 2026); [Publications du Québec, French book titles and ranges](https://www.publicationsduquebec.gouv.qc.ca/lois-et-reglements/codifications-administratives/code-civil-du-quebec/) (separately dated administrative consolidation, used here as structural cross-check); [LégisQuébec French electronic statute and versions](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/CCQ-1991). Use the applicable version, not the summary below, for a particular question.

Read the preliminary provision before the books; the final provisions follow Book X. Book IV starts at 898.1 (its first title begins at 899). Decimal-numbered articles are not page numbers.

## Recommended learning sequence: start with evidence

1. **Book VII (Evidence):** Begin with arts. 2803–2805; separate the burden on each disputed proposition, the civil standard and the presumption of good faith. Then learn means of proof (starting at 2811) and admissibility (starting at 2857). Treat admissibility, probative value and ultimate sufficiency as three separate questions. Use source-of-proof exercises with neutral facts before comparing contested documents.
2. **Book V (Obligations), then VIII (Prescription):** First identify the rule creating the right, duties, breach and remedy; then check what the passage of time may do to it. Prescription is not the same as a procedural period for a filing, service or appeal.
3. **Books I, II and III:** Study who acts and in what capacity before turning to family regimes and succession. Note overlapping federal or specialized legislation and the need to verify applicable transitional rules.
4. **Books IV, VI and IX:** Follow one property example from the right itself, to a creditor's security or priority, to publication and third-person effects. Do not assume registration creates every right or resolves every priority question.
5. **Book X:** Use cross-border scenarios only after distinguishing international jurisdiction, applicable law, and recognition/enforcement.

## Verify the edition and dates

- Record the article, paragraph, official URL, consolidation date, and date checked. Identify the event or legal issue before picking an applicable version: events within the same matter may call for different dates.
- In LégisQuébec, check the article's versions and read the full statute at the relevant date. Verify commencement and transitional text in the amending Act or order where the effective date matters; a change's adoption, consolidation, and entry into force need not coincide.
- Check relevant cross-references in context. If applying a special statute, a regulation or the C.p.c., identify the exact source and explain how it is related before borrowing its rules. Use constitutional, federal or special Québec legislation where applicable rather than assuming the C.c.Q. settles everything.
- For case-law propositions, open the actual reasons, cite a supporting paragraph and check subsequent history. If the current or historical text, applicable date, or necessary authority is not verified, flag the gap. Do not infer applicability from a PDF footer alone.

## Reusable lesson template

1. **Study question:** One narrow question, with Book and official article citation.
2. **Source and version:** Official link, article/paragraph, consolidation, date relevant to the example, and any special legislation, exceptions or open version checks.
3. **Rule in plain language:** Separate rule, conditions and exceptions. Show which statement comes from the text and which is an application or inference.
4. **Bilingual terminology:** Official French term, official English counterpart, and optional Mandarin clarification. Mark a pedagogical translation as such; do not present it as an official text.
5. **Fictional example:** Give one short fact pattern and a realistic contrasting variant. Distinguish material facts, competing inferences and unknowns. Do not import private case details.
6. **Apply and prove:** Identify the material proposition, which party must prove it under the applicable rule, what each item supports and where the evidence is insufficient. State any procedural cross-reference separately.
7. **Self-check:** Ask one question learners can answer from the rule; give a short reasoned answer and one next reading. If the exercise concerns a time limit, ask first for the controlling rule and trigger, not just a date.
