# Québec Civil Procedure Skill

A plain-language educational guide under Québec's [Code of Civil Procedure, CQLR c C-25.01](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/C-25.01) (C.C.P.). It is not legal advice and cannot establish a filing deadline or jurisdiction without the relevant facts and current rules. The article references in this revision were checked against the official October 15, 2026 consolidation; check the version in force for the relevant event before applying them.

## How to use this skill

1. Identify the proceeding, court/division, judicial district, amount and remedy, status of each party, and any special statute or order. Do not assume that the general civil track applies to a tax contestation, administrative appeal, family matter, residential lease matter, or small claim.
2. Give a short answer, then state the source, exceptions, and first actionable step. Check the official Code, regulations, court rules and forms, and any case-specific directions. If a necessary fact or source is missing, identify it, show conditional branches and do not supply an unverified deadline or filing instruction.
3. Distinguish documents drafted, filed, served or notified, and evidence admitted. For deadlines, record both the prescribed act and the event that starts the clock. An email date, filing date, service date, notice of judgment and judgment at the hearing are not automatically interchangeable.
4. Explain options without taking over a user's case. Keep personal identifiers and sensitive documents out of public examples; do not send or file anything without a separate instruction. Court staff can help locate forms and explain registry procedures; seek timely qualified legal help when the deadline, representation, jurisdiction or remedy is uncertain.

## 1. Identify the forum and applicable track

Start with [Code structure and forums](references/ccp-structure-and-forums.md). Check exclusive statutory or tribunal jurisdiction before monetary thresholds. Do not hard-code amounts where indexing or special rules apply. For ordinary Court of Québec civil jurisdiction, art. 35 excludes interest from the threshold calculation and distinguishes exclusive and concurrent jurisdiction. Superior Court is the general first-instance court within art. 33, subject to matters assigned elsewhere. Injunction and supervisory jurisdiction have their own provisions; check arts. 34 and 509 and the governing statute.

Small Claims is a distinct Court of Québec track. Check arts. 536–542 for claim type, amount, party eligibility, representation and exceptions. Do not suggest splitting one claim to bypass the limit. In other contentious proceedings legal persons are generally required to be represented by counsel (art. 87); small claims has distinct rules.

## 2. Check prescription and pre-filing steps

Prescription is separate from a procedural time limit. Identify the cause of action, dates, applicable legislation and any interruption or suspension before assuming the general three-year rule for personal or movable real rights in [Civil Code of Québec art. 2925](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/CCQ-1991). Do not assume that a demand letter stops prescription. Default and formal notice are separate questions; see arts. 1594–1597 C.C.Q. Consider private dispute prevention and resolution before going to court (art. 1 C.C.P.); do not let a settlement discussion obscure a live deadline. Observe proportionality and cooperation duties (arts. 18, 20 C.C.P.).

## 3. Prepare and transmit the initiating documents

Use the current official forms and local directions. For an ordinary contentious action, distinguish filing the originating application from serving it and the summons on the opposing party (arts. 107, 139, 141–147 C.C.P.). Service by bailiff is ordinarily required for the originating application; check exceptions and keep proof of the method actually used. Later notification may follow a different rule. In Small Claims the clerk notifies the defendant under art. 546; do not instruct the claimant to substitute the ordinary service procedure without verifying the specific rule.

For Title I.1 simplified proceedings, no case protocol is required under art. 535.2. Article 535.3 limits statements in the originating application to five pages, with possible later court authorization on serious grounds. Check the applicability of art. 535.1 first; do not assume every case filed in the Court of Québec follows this title.

## 4. Calendar the right pre-trial steps

Use [Deadlines and checklists](references/deadlines-and-checklists.md). Verify the service documents and actual triggering dates. Distinguish:

- **Ordinary proceedings:** the answer under arts. 145–147; the general case protocol under arts. 148–150 when required; and the plaintiff's strict setting-down step under arts. 173–177. The 45-day protocol period runs from service of the summons in the ordinary art. 149 setting; family cases and other procedural situations differ.
- **Title I.1 simplified proceedings:** plaintiff's exhibit disclosure and filing under art. 535.4 (20 days after service of the summons); written disclosure and filing of preliminary exceptions and incidental applications under art. 535.5 (filing within 45 days after service of the summons); defendant's argument outline, notice and exhibit disclosure under art. 535.6 (95 days after service of the summons); and the clerk's distinct setting down under art. 535.13. The ordinary case protocol and plaintiff's ordinary setting-down timetable are not substitutes for these rules.
- **Evidence:** Identify what each fact needs for proof, disclose and file as the applicable rules and court orders require, and track objections separately. Check arts. 221–230 for pre-trial examinations and the special rules in arts. 535.4, 535.6, 535.8–535.10 when applicable. Article 229 generally bars pre-trial examinations where the amount or value claimed is below $50,000; verify exceptions and the applicable track. For expert evidence check arts. 231–241 and any applicable court order; art. 535.10 is about origin or integrity of filed evidence, not joint experts.

## 5. Prepare for hearing, judgment and enforcement

Build a chronology, an issue-to-evidence list, and focused questions. Follow the court's instructions for copies and filing; for a subpoena check art. 269 and any authorized shorter notice. Keep a copy of the decision, record whether it was rendered at the hearing, and preserve the notice of judgment.

- **Appeal:** Check first whether the judgment is appealable and whether leave is required (arts. 30–32, 351–357). Under the general rule in art. 360, the notice of appeal is filed within 30 days after the date of the notice of judgment, or after the judgment date if rendered at the hearing. If leave is required, file both documents together. Read arts. 361–363 and any special statute for different or strict time limits. Small Claims judgments are final and not appealable (art. 564); do not confuse appeal with a distinct remedy.
- **Enforcement:** Verify finality, any appeal or stay, and the waiting period under art. 656 before taking steps. Small Claims has special waiting periods that differ for a judgment following failure to answer the summons and other judgments; check the exact text. Do not assume that a judgment is self-executing or that a single period applies to all cases.
