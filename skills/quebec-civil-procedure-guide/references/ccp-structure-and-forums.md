# Québec Code of Civil Procedure: Structure and Choice of Forum

Source: [Official English Code of Civil Procedure, CQLR, c. C-25.01](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/pdf/cs/C-25.01%20.pdf), consolidation updated October 15, 2026 for the article references below. Before filing, confirm the text applicable on the relevant date in [LégisQuébec](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/C-25.01), any transitional provisions, and applicable court rules and orders. Educational reference only.

## The eight Books

| Book | Subject | Article range |
|---|---|---|
| I | General framework: consider private dispute resolution before going to court, procedural principles, jurisdiction, court offices, pleadings, notification and service. Article 1 requires consideration, not universal mandatory mediation. | 1–140 |
| II | Contentious proceedings: originating application, answer, case management, contestation, preparation, evidence and trial. Article 141 makes Book II the baseline; Books V and VI can supplement it or depart from it. | 141–301 |
| III | Non-contentious proceedings: separate procedure, not merely a lawsuit the other party has chosen not to defend. Check the nature of the application (art. 302). | 302–320 |
| IV | Judgment, legal costs, revocation and appeal. Revocation is not interchangeable with appeal. | 321–390 |
| V | Special rules for certain civil matters, including persons, family, successions, property, security, evidence and private international law. | 391–508 |
| VI | Special procedures: injunctions, seizure before judgment, judicial review, special rules for qualifying Court of Québec cases, small claims and class actions. | 509–604 |
| VII | Private dispute resolution: mediation and arbitration. Check art. 622 for the effect of a valid arbitration agreement covering an issue. | 605–655 |
| VIII | Execution: voluntary performance, compulsory execution, seizure, judicial sale and distribution. | 656–777 |

## Choose the forum before choosing the procedure

1. **Identify the relief and governing statute first.** Check whether an enactment assigns the subject exclusively to a court or tribunal or creates a specific appeal, review or objection mechanism. A statutory appeal is not an original civil action; its enabling Act may control standing, trigger, service and time limit. For example, see the [Tax Administration Act](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/A-6.002), s. 93.1.10 and related provisions, for the Court of Québec tax assessment appeal track after objection; consult the distinct statutory summary appeal rules where potentially applicable. Do not use C.C.P. art. 360 as the deadline for filing a first-stage statutory appeal from an administrative decision.
2. **Superior Court (arts. 33–34).** It has residual original jurisdiction over applications not formally and exclusively assigned elsewhere, and exclusive jurisdiction over class actions and applications for an injunction (art. 33). Article 34, not art. 33, provides general judicial review power, subject to its statutory limits. Check arts. 529–535 for the application for judicial review. Do not say all family matters are exclusively in Superior Court: check arts. 37–37.2 and the applicable family, youth or transitional legislation.
3. **Court of Québec, ordinary civil monetary jurisdiction (art. 35).** The October 15, 2026 consolidation prints **less than $75,000** as the exclusive band and **$75,000 to less than $100,000** as concurrent with Superior Court, at the plaintiff’s option, **excluding interest**. These are not a permanent filing table. Article 35(4) provides CPI-based adjustment, increases in $5,000 increments when the statutory conditions are met, September 1 effective dates, a notice in the [Gazette officielle du Québec](https://www.publicationsduquebec.gouv.qc.ca/gazette-officielle/), and continuity for applications introduced before the relevant September 1. Check the Gazette and applicable filing date, including on the apparent boundary. The [official Court of Québec overview](https://www.quebec.ca/justice-et-etat-civil/systeme-judiciaire/tribunaux-du-quebec/cour-du-quebec/apercu) reports the same bands. Article 35 does not apply where jurisdiction is formally and exclusively assigned elsewhere or in family matters. Also check later cross-applications, amendments and multiple plaintiffs under art. 35. Small claims is a track within the Court of Québec, not an independent lower monetary cutoff for that Court’s jurisdiction.
4. **Court of Québec special civil rules (Book VI, Title I.1).** Under art. 535.1 of the cited edition, the special rules supplement Book II for proceedings brought in the Court of Québec under art. 35 with an amount claimed or value below $100,000 excluding interest, including specified related applications. Check the text of art. 535.1 independently of the indexed art. 35 jurisdiction limits. Do not assume these rules apply to every case before the Court of Québec, to the same-dollar case brought in Superior Court, or to an administrative appeal. Examples: no case protocol (art. 535.2); five-page limit on the statements in the originating application, subject to the court’s serious-grounds exception (art. 535.3); art. 229 bars pre-trial examination below $50,000, not every kind of evidence disclosure; in this special track, art. 535.9 limits written examinations to three pages and, at $50,000 or more, normally allows each party one oral examination unless the court decides otherwise. Check arts. 535.4–535.15 for the governing timetable and exceptions.
5. **Small Claims Division (Book VI, Title II).** Arts. 536–538 set the scope, exclusions and reduction rules: qualifying recovery claims not exceeding $15,000 excluding interest; qualifying contract resolution, resiliation or cancellation only if neither contract value nor any amount claimed exceeds that threshold. Qualifying organizations have at most 10 employees under their direction or control at any time in the preceding 12 months. Art. 537 excludes claims arising from dwelling leases, support or alleged defamation, and certain claims assigned for payment. A voluntary reduction is possible, but generally do not split a larger claim (art. 538, including its limited exceptions). A natural person generally self-represents, subject to permitted unpaid mandates; legal persons and other listed entities have different representation rules. An exceptional lawyer authorization for a complex point of law is possible under art. 542; consulting a lawyer is permitted. The judgment is not appealable (art. 564); revocation is distinct (art. 568). For contested claims of $5,000 or less excluding interest, verify the mandatory mediation and automatic arbitration rules, exceptions, consent and any right to refuse; see art. 570, the [governing regulation](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/rc/C-25.01%2C%20r.%200.6.1), and the Québec government’s [mediation](https://www.quebec.ca/justice-et-etat-civil/petites-creances/mediation-arbitrage-petites-creances/mediation-petites-creances) and [arbitration](https://www.quebec.ca/justice-et-etat-civil/petites-creances/mediation-arbitrage-petites-creances/arbitrage) guidance. Never route a statutory tax summary appeal to ordinary small claims solely because of its amount.
6. **Tribunal administratif du logement (TAL).** Check the [Act respecting the Administrative Housing Tribunal](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/en/document/cs/T-15.01), s. 28 for original jurisdiction and ss. 91–92 for the special appeal-to-Court-of-Québec route (leave, exclusions and time limit). A dwelling-lease dispute is not an ordinary small-claims action (C.C.P. art. 537). If a housing claim exceeds a monetary limit, read s. 28 and its express exceptions rather than assume the answer.
7. **Territory and representation are separate checks.** After selecting the court, check the judicial district under arts. 40–48 or special legislation. The defendant’s domicile is the ordinary starting point at first instance (art. 41); check the exceptions and added options, including contract formation (art. 42) and consumer or employment contracts (art. 43). Check arts. 87–88 for lawyer or notary requirements and exceptions; legal persons generally require counsel in ordinary contentious proceedings, unlike the special small-claims representation rules under art. 542.

## Court of Appeal: as of right, with leave, or unavailable?

- **Arts. 29–30:** Determine the appellate court and whether the judgment ends the proceeding or falls within another category appealable as of right. Read all art. 30 leave categories, not just the value test. If the **subject matter in dispute on appeal** is below $60,000, leave is required under art. 30(1) of the second paragraph; apply the last paragraph’s valuation rule, which accounts for accrued interest and the additional indemnity and disregards legal costs, subject to the bodily-injury qualification. Do not substitute the original claim’s face amount, or the art. 35 first-instance thresholds.
- **Arts. 31–32:** An interlocutory or case-management ruling is not necessarily immediately appealable. Check the narrow types appealable as of right, leave criteria and prohibition or limits before assuming an appeal is available. Article 31 calls for appeal without delay where applicable.
- **Arts. 352, 357–358, 360–363:** Under the ordinary C.C.P. rule, the notice of appeal is **filed within 30 days after the date of the notice of judgment**, or after the judgment date if rendered at the hearing; when leave is required, the notice is **filed together with** the leave application, not replaced by it. Check service and notification within the appeal period, shorter periods in art. 361, separate incidental appeal timing in art. 360, the strict limits and any exceptional relief under art. 363, and any special statute. Article 564 excludes appeals from Small Claims Division judgments under that Title.
