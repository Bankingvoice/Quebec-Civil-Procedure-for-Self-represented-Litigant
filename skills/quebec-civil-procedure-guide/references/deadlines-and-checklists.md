# Québec civil procedure: deadlines and practical checklists

Use the [official Code of Civil Procedure, CQLR c C‑25.01](https://www.legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fr/document/lc/C-25.01?langCont=en) in force at the relevant event. The article references below were checked against the Québec Official Publisher consolidation updated October 15, 2026. Check special legislation, court rules, practice directions and case-specific orders. A filing, a notification, a bailiff service and presenting an application to the court are different steps. If the correct track, triggering event or date is unknown, identify the missing information rather than announcing a deadline.

## How to count a procedural time limit

- Under art. 83, where expressed in days, exclude the triggering day, include the terminal day and count intervening weekends and holidays. Where expressed in months, use the corresponding calendar number in the last month, or that month's last day if there is no matching date.
- Under art. 83, a time limit expires at midnight on the last day; if it would normally end on a Saturday or a holiday, it extends to the following working day. Check the applicable legal holiday definition and filing or notification rules, including office hours and platform cut-offs; midnight is not a promise that a particular filing channel will accept the document.
- Art. 82 concerns court sittings; periods when certain hearings need not be held are not a blanket suspension of time limits. Art. 84 distinguishes strict and other time limits. Do not assume an extension or rely on one without verifying the specific rule and any order.

## Common clocks by track

| Track or action | Rule to verify in the applicable text |
|---|---|
| Ordinary answer to summons | Generally answer within 15 days after service, notify the plaintiff or the plaintiff's lawyer as applicable, and file with the court office. Default judgment may follow, but is not automatic (arts. 145–147, 175, 180). |
| Ordinary case protocol, when required | Art. 148 concerns the protocol's contents. Under art. 149, file within 45 days after service of the summons, or within three months in family matters, subject to applicable special rules. Art. 150 addresses acceptance, including deemed acceptance. |
| Ordinary setting down for trial and judgment | Under art. 173, the plaintiff must ready the case and file the request within the strict six months (one year in family matters) after deemed acceptance or court acceptance/establishment of the protocol. If the required protocol or proposed protocol was not timely filed, generally count from service of the application; extension in that situation requires actual impossibility. Arts. 173–174 and 176–177 address exceptions, format and consequences. If the plaintiff misses the strict deadline, there is presumed discontinuance unless another party files within 30 days after expiry; the court can also relieve the plaintiff upon actual impossibility. This is not a deadline for the trial to be held. |
| Court of Québec, qualifying special simplified track | No case protocol is required (arts. 535.1–535.2). The originating application's statements are limited to five pages unless the court exceptionally authorizes later supplemental pages on serious grounds (art. 535.3). This track is not the same as Small Claims. |
| Simplified track: plaintiff's completion | Within 20 days after service of the summons, disclose supporting exhibits to the defendant and file notice of the nature and number of intended affidavit testimonies, pre-trial examinations and expert opinions (art. 535.4). |
| Simplified track: preliminary exceptions or incidental applications | Disclose them in writing to the other party and file the written disclosure within 45 days after service of the summons. The other party may make written representations within 10 days after disclosure. Matters that could not be disclosed in time are presented as soon as possible (art. 535.5). |
| Simplified track: defence outline and exhibits | Within 95 days after service of the summons, the defendant files a brief outline of arguments and notice of the nature and number of intended affidavit testimonies, pre-trial examinations and expert opinions, and discloses supporting exhibits to plaintiff. The outline's statements are limited to two pages, or seven with a cross-application, unless the court exceptionally authorizes later supplemental pages on serious grounds (art. 535.6). |
| Simplified track: settlement and setting down | Settlement conference generally 130–160 days from service of the summons; art. 535.12 specifies when a pre-trial conference can replace it. The court clerk sets the case down on a court order (including at case management or pre-trial conference) or no later than six months after service of the summons (art. 535.13). Do not import the plaintiff-driven art. 173 mechanism into this timeline. |
| Small Claims defendant's choice | Clerk notifies the application, options notice and exhibit list. Indicate the chosen option to the clerk within 20 days after notification; otherwise judgment may be rendered without further notice or extension (arts. 546–547, 552). |
| Witness subpoena, ordinary proceedings | Generally call the witness at least 10 days before scheduled attendance. In urgent circumstances a judge or clerk may shorten the period to at least 24 hours, with the decision recorded on the subpoena. Check separate rules for confined witnesses and Small Claims (arts. 269, 557). |
| Application for revocation | Check grounds separately (arts. 345–346). Serve every party within 30 days after the cause preventing a defence ceased, or after becoming aware of the judgment, evidence or fact constituting grounds, as applicable; check the rule for a minor. Then present within 30 days after service and no more than six months after judgment. These are strict time limits (art. 347). |
| Ordinary appeal to the Court of Appeal | Generally file notice of appeal within 30 days after the date of the notice of judgment, or after the date of the judgment if rendered at the hearing. If leave is required, file the leave application with the notice. Verify service, proof of service and other steps separately (arts. 139, 352–354, 357–358, 360). Check special shorter limits, successor rules, and the strict-limit exception (arts. 361–363). Small Claims judgments cannot be appealed (art. 564); administrative and statutory appeals can have different rules. |
| Forced execution; Small Claims special timing | Normally the debtor must have failed to comply voluntarily and judgment must be final, subject to provisional execution and special rules. For Small Claims, wait 30 days after judgment was rendered, except a default judgment following failure to answer the summons, attend a case management conference or defend on the merits, for which the period is 10 days after rendering. Check whether a court order sets a different period (arts. 565, 656, 660–661). |

## Before filing

- [ ] Check the applicable limitation period, trigger, suspension/interruption and any separate advance notice, rather than assuming three years. Check urgently if filing or service may be time-sensitive.
- [ ] Identify the correct party and legal capacity; verify an organization's exact name through Québec's enterprise register if relevant.
- [ ] Check whether a formal demand or special notice is required or useful. Record the demand and proof of delivery if used; do not assume sending it suspends prescription.
- [ ] Identify the eligible forum, judicial district, representation rule, current monetary thresholds and applicable forms or court directions. Do not assume a court based only on amount.
- [ ] Keep a dated chronology and indexed evidence. Track when a document was shared with the other side, filed with the court, and accepted into evidence separately.

## Before the hearing

- [ ] Check the court's directions for copies or electronic exhibits, filing deadlines and format. Bring or arrange access to working copies as permitted.
- [ ] Bring relevant proof of notification or bailiff service; confirm which exhibits were exchanged and filed.
- [ ] Keep a one-page chronology and exhibit index so you can locate the record quickly.
- [ ] Confirm witnesses, interpretation or accessibility arrangements, courthouse or connection details, and any subpoenas and service records.
- [ ] Plan to arrive early, check the courtroom roll and silence your phone. These are preparation tips, not statutory conditions.
