# Deadlines, Time Computation, and Litigant Checklists

## How to Count Deadlines in Quebec (Art. 83 C.C.P.)

1. **Do not count the starting day:** Day 1 is the day *after* the triggering event (e.g., the day after bailiff service or notice of judgment).
2. **Count calendar days (including weekends):** Saturdays, Sundays, and holidays are counted during the period.
3. **Include the final day—unless it is a non-juridical day:** If the final day lands on a Saturday, Sunday, or statutory holiday (art. 82 C.C.P.), the deadline rolls forward to the **next working day**.
4. **Summer and winter recesses:** Court offices remain open, though certain trial hearings pause between July 1–August 31 and December 21–January 6 (art. 81 C.C.P.). Deadlines for pleadings generally keep running unless suspended by law or court order.

## Critical Procedural Deadlines

| Stage | Deadline | C.C.P. Reference | Consequence of Missing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Defendant's Answer (*Réponse*)** | **15 days** after service of Summons | Art. 147 | Plaintiff can note default and apply for default judgment (art. 180). |
| **Small Claims Contest / Options** | **20 days** after receipt of notice from clerk | Art. 548 | Plaintiff may obtain judgment by default without a full contested hearing. |
| **Case Protocol (*Protocole de l'instance*)** | **45 days** after service of Summons (when required) | Art. 148 | Court may impose a schedule or penalize non-cooperating party. |
| **Simplified Track Preliminary Exceptions** | **45 days** after service of Originating Application | Art. 535.4 | Right to raise preliminary procedural objections may be forfeited. |
| **Simplified Track Defence & Exhibits** | **95 days** after service of Originating Application | Art. 535.6 | Case may proceed without the defendant's written defence. |
| **Trial Readiness (*Inscription*)** | Within **6 months** (or **1 year** in family matters) from Case Protocol | Art. 173 | Case is deemed discontinued unless an extension is granted by the court. |
| **Subpoenaing a Witness** | At least **10 days** before the hearing | Art. 269 | Witness cannot be compelled to attend if notified too late. |
| **Application for Revocation of Default Judgment** | **30 days** from knowledge of judgment (max 6 months from judgment) | Art. 347 | Default judgment becomes final and enforceable. |
| **Appeal (Court of Appeal)** | **30 days** from date of notice of judgment | Art. 360 | Right of appeal is lost (except rare extensions under art. 363). |

## Pre-Filing Checklist (Plaintiff)

- [ ] **Prescription verified:** Confirm the 3-year (or shorter applicable) limitation period has not expired.
- [ ] **Right defendant identified:** Checked personal full name or exact corporate legal name on the *Registraire des entreprises du Québec* (REQ).
- [ ] **Formal notice (*mise en demeure*) sent:** Sent written demand with a clear deadline and kept proof of delivery.
- [ ] **Correct court chosen:** Verified claim amount and subject matter against Small Claims ($15k), Court of Québec (< $75k / $100k), Superior Court ($100k+), or TAL.
- [ ] **Evidence organized:** Gathered contracts, invoices, emails, photos, and receipts; labeled `P-1`, `P-2`, etc.

## Hearing Day Checklist

- [ ] **3 complete sets of exhibits:** 1 for the judge, 1 for the opposing party, 1 for yourself, indexed by exhibit number (`P-1` / `D-1`).
- [ ] **Proof of notification:** Bailiff returns or registered mail receipts showing the other side received all pleadings and exhibits.
- [ ] **One-page chronology:** Bullet-point timeline of key dates, amounts, and exhibit references so you do not lose your place under pressure.
- [ ] **Witnesses confirmed:** Verified attendance and brought proof of subpoena service (`citation à comparaître`) if any witness was formally summoned.
- [ ] **Courtroom logistics:** Arrive 30 minutes early, check the courtroom roll (*rôle d'audience*), silence your phone, and bring pen and paper for notes.
